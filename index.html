Strategic watchlist

  • Energy & shipping: Strait of Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, Red Sea and oil supply.
  • Civilian protection: attacks on civilians, hospitals, schools and displacement.
  • Diplomacy: ceasefire talks, negotiations, mediation and breakdowns.
  • Regional spillover: cross-border strikes, airspace closures and military mobilization.
  • Economic effects: oil prices, trade routes, sanctions and transport disruption.

How to read the index

The 0–100 score is calculated from the volume of matching reports and escalation-related terms in the returned headlines. More coverage can mean more events, but it can also mean more media attention.

Use it to notice changes, not as a probability of war. Compare several days and read the linked sources before drawing conclusions.

Breaking reports may change. Casualty figures, responsibility claims, and battlefield statements can be disputed or revised.

Verification checklist

A simple standard for reading reports responsibly.

  1. Check the original publisher, author and publication time.
  2. Look for confirmation from a second independent newsroom or primary institution.
  3. Separate confirmed facts from claims by governments, militaries or armed groups.
  4. For images/videos, check the original upload, location, date and possible reuse from older events.
  5. If sources disagree, mark the issue as disputed rather than choosing the most dramatic claim.
